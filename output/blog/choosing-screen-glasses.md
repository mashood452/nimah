# How to Choose Glasses for Long Screen Days

If your eyes feel tired by the end of a day at a screen, the right glasses can help. "Right" means the correct prescription for your screen distance, a comfortable frame and sensible coatings, not one single feature.

The American Academy of Ophthalmology (AAO) describes [computer glasses](https://www.aao.org/eye-health/tips-prevention/computer-usage) as prescription glasses that focus your eyes at screen distance, about 20 to 26 inches from your face, and it says they are not the same as blue-light-blocking glasses. This guide takes you through the choices in the order that matters most.

## Start with an eye test

Tired, achy or dry eyes are the classic signs of digital eye strain, and the AAO notes that the symptoms are no different from those of long stretches of reading or other close work. A small change in prescription, or a need for reading help that you have not noticed, can add to them. The [American Optometric Association](https://www.aoa.org/healthy-eyes/eye-and-vision-conditions/computer-vision-syndrome) says that even people who already wear glasses or contact lenses may find their prescription is not suitable for the specific viewing distance of their computer screen.

Tell your optician how far your screen is from your eyes, how many hours you spend at it and whether you use more than one screen. That helps them choose lenses for the way you actually work.

## Choose lenses for how you work

| Your situation | Lens to ask about | Why it helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| You need one distance of correction and work at one screen distance | [Single vision lenses](/lenses/single-vision) set for your screen distance | The lens is made for the distance you look at most |
| You need help at several distances, such as the screen, documents and across the room | [Progressive](/lenses/progressive) or office-style lenses | They cover more than one distance, and office designs are made with a wider zone for screens |
| You do not need vision correction | Clear lenses with coatings, in a frame you like | They will not change how your eyes focus, so treat them as a comfort and style choice |

The AAO notes that some computer glasses have multifocal lenses to help you shift focus between close, intermediate and far distances. Your optician can tell you which design suits your prescription. ## Add an anti-glare coating

Reflections from screens and overhead lights make your eyes work harder. The AAO advises reducing glare on your screen, for example with a matte screen filter, and an [anti-glare coating](/lenses/anti-glare) reduces reflections from the surface of your lenses. Many people find it the most noticeable improvement for desk work.

## What about blue-cut lenses?

Blue-cut lenses filter part of the blue light from screens. The AAO does not recommend blue-light-blocking glasses for computer use, and a [Cochrane review](https://www.cochrane.org/CD013244) found they may not reduce short-term eye strain. Treat them as an optional comfort extra, not as the main reason to choose a pair.

## Pick a frame that fits

You may wear these glasses for hours, so fit matters as much as style. Check that:

- The frame is light, especially if your lenses are strong.
- It sits comfortably on your nose without slipping when you look down.
- It does not pinch at your temples or behind your ears.
- Your eyes sit near the centre of each lens, and the lenses are tall enough to look at the screen through comfortably.
- It still feels comfortable after you have worn it for longer than a few minutes.

## Set up your screen too

Even the best glasses cannot replace good habits. The AAO's advice for computer use is to:

- Sit about 25 inches (arm's length) from the screen, with the screen positioned so you gaze slightly downward.
- Take regular breaks with the 20-20-20 rule: every 20 minutes, look at something at least 20 feet away for at least 20 seconds.
- Blink often. People normally blink about 15 times a minute but only 5 to 7 times a minute at a screen.
- Match screen brightness to the room, and try increasing the contrast.
- Use artificial tears if your eyes feel dry.

If you wear contact lenses, the AAO notes that long periods on digital devices can make dry eye an ongoing problem, and suggests giving your eyes a break by wearing your glasses instead. Our [contact lens guide](/contact-lenses) covers safe wear and care.

## Before you buy: a quick checklist

1. Book an eye test, and tell your optician your screen distance and how long you work.
2. Choose single vision, office-style or progressive lenses for the way you work.
3. Add an anti-glare coating.
4. Treat blue-cut lenses as optional.
5. Check that the frame is light and stays comfortable for hours.

> General information only :: This article is not medical advice. If your eyes are painful or red, your vision changes, or tired eyes do not improve with breaks, see an eye care professional.

## Common questions about glasses for screen use

### Do I need special glasses for computer work?

Not always. If your eyes are comfortable and your prescription is up to date, you may not. If screens leave your eyes tired, book an eye test and ask about lenses for your screen distance.

### Are computer glasses the same as blue-light glasses?

No. The AAO says computer glasses for reducing eye strain are not the same as blue-light-blocking glasses. Computer glasses are made to focus your eyes at screen distance.

### Can I get computer glasses without a prescription?

If you do not need vision correction, you can choose clear lenses with an anti-glare coating, or blue-cut lenses, in a frame you like. They will not change how your eyes focus, so see an optician first if you have symptoms.

### How far should my screen be from my eyes?

The AAO suggests about 25 inches, around arm's length, with the screen positioned so you gaze slightly downward.

### Do progressive lenses work for screens?

They can, but some people find the screen-distance zone of an ordinary progressive narrow. That is why office-style designs exist. Ask your optician which design suits you.

### When should I see an eye care professional?

If your eyes are painful or red, your vision changes, or tiredness does not ease with breaks and good screen habits, see an eye care professional.
