# What Is Blue Light, and Do Blue-Cut Glasses Help?

Blue light is part of the normal light around you, and the sun is by far its biggest source. Screens give off some too, which is why blue-cut glasses are often sold for long days at a laptop or phone.

The short answer is that the [American Academy of Ophthalmology (AAO) says there is no scientific evidence that blue light from digital devices damages your eyes](https://www.aao.org/eye-health/tips-prevention/should-you-be-worried-about-blue-light), and it does not recommend special blue-light-blocking eyewear for computer use. A [Cochrane review of 17 studies](https://www.cochrane.org/CD013244) found that blue-light filtering lenses may not reduce short-term eye strain from computer work. If your eyes feel tired after screen time, how you use your screens matters more than the colour of the light.

## What is blue light?

Visible light is made of many colours, each with its own wavelength. Blue light sits at the short-wavelength end of the visible range, so it carries a little more energy than light at the red end. That is why it gets so much attention.

Blue light is not a modern invention or a sign that something is wrong. It is a normal part of daylight, and it affects our body clock. The AAO explains that during the day blue light wakes us up and stimulates us, which matters again when we get to sleep below.

## Where does blue light come from?

| Source | How much blue light | Good to know |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The sun | By far the largest source | It also carries ultraviolet (UV) light, which is the bigger risk for your eyes outdoors |
| LED and fluorescent lighting | A smaller source | Used in many homes and offices |
| Screens, including phones, laptops, tablets, TVs and consoles | Much less than the sun | The AAO says the exposure is no more damaging than blue light from the sun |

The AAO points out that blue light exposure from screens is much less than from the sun, and that too much UV exposure from sunlight raises the risk of eye diseases. For time outdoors, the protection that counts is a pair of [sunglasses with UV400 protection](/sunglasses/uv400). Our guide to [choosing sunglasses](/blog/choose-sunglasses) explains what to check.

## Is blue light from screens harmful?

Alarmist headlines claim that blue light from screens is blinding us, but the AAO is clear: there is no scientific evidence that blue light from digital devices causes damage to your eye. It also reports that [the amount of light from a computer has never been shown to cause any eye disease](https://www.aao.org/eye-health/tips-prevention/are-computer-glasses-worth-it), and that a study found no measurable UVA or UVB radiation coming from computer screens.

The discomfort many people feel after a long day at a screen is real, though. The AAO says it is most likely digital eye strain, which does not cause lasting damage and is easily preventable.

## What does the research say about blue-cut glasses?

Blue-cut glasses, also called blue-light blocking or blue-light filtering glasses, have been tested in clinical trials. The best summary is a [Cochrane review](https://www.cochrane.org/CD013244) of 17 studies with 619 people in six countries, covering research published up to March 2022. It found:

- **Eye strain:** there may be no short-term advantage from blue-light filtering lenses for visual fatigue with computer use, compared with lenses that do not filter blue light.
- **Sleep:** the evidence on sleep is inconclusive, and the effects are unclear.
- **Side effects:** harmful effects were temporary and generally mild, and similar effects were reported with ordinary lenses.

The AAO reaches a similar view. It says several studies suggest blue-light-blocking glasses do not improve symptoms of digital eye strain, and it [does not recommend them](https://www.aao.org/eye-health/tips-prevention/are-computer-glasses-worth-it).

## Blue light, sleep and screens at night

There is some evidence that blue light affects the body's circadian rhythm, our natural wake and sleep cycle. The AAO says that too much blue light late at night from a phone, tablet or computer can make it harder to get to sleep, and it suggests limiting screen time in the two to three hours before bed. Using a night or dark mode on your devices in the evening can help too.

Whether blue-cut lenses help with sleep is not settled, because the Cochrane review found the evidence inconclusive. Changing when and how you use screens at night is the step the AAO recommends.

## Why do my eyes feel tired after screen time?

The most common cause is digital eye strain, which is linked to how we use screens rather than to the colour of the light.

- **We blink less.** People normally blink about 15 times a minute, but only about 5 to 7 times a minute at a computer or other digital screen, according to the [AAO](https://www.aao.org/eye-health/tips-prevention/computer-usage). That can leave eyes dry, achy and uncomfortable.
- **We focus at one close distance for hours.** The AAO notes that the symptoms are no different from those of long stretches of reading, writing or other close work.
- **Brightness and glare make the eyes work harder.** A screen that glows much brighter than the room is harder to look at.
- **An out-of-date prescription adds to it.** The [American Optometric Association](https://www.aoa.org/healthy-eyes/eye-and-vision-conditions/computer-vision-syndrome) says that even people who already have a prescription may find it is not suitable for the specific viewing distance of their computer screen.

## What actually helps tired eyes

| Habit | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Take regular breaks | Follow the 20-20-20 rule: every 20 minutes, look at something at least 20 feet (6 metres) away for at least 20 seconds |
| Blink on purpose | Make a conscious effort to blink often. A reminder note on the screen can help |
| Sit at a comfortable distance | About 25 inches (arm's length) from the screen, with the screen positioned so you gaze slightly downward |
| Match brightness to the room | Adjust screen brightness to the light around you, and try increasing the contrast |
| Reduce glare | Use good lighting, a matte screen filter if needed, and an [anti-glare coating](/lenses/anti-glare) on your glasses |
| Refresh dry eyes | Artificial tears can help when your eyes feel dry |
| Have an eye test | Check that your prescription suits the distance you work at |

Most of these cost nothing, and they work whether or not you wear blue-cut lenses. They follow the AAO's advice on [computer use and your eyes](https://www.aao.org/eye-health/tips-prevention/computer-usage).

## So, are blue-cut glasses worth it?

Blue-cut glasses are a comfort option, not a medical treatment, and the evidence does not show that they prevent eye strain or protect against eye disease. If you like how they feel during long days at a screen, there is little downside to choosing them, as long as you do not expect them to replace breaks, good lighting and an up-to-date prescription.

## Common questions about blue light and blue-cut glasses

### Can blue light from screens cause permanent eye damage?

The AAO says there is no scientific evidence that blue light from digital devices damages your eyes. Digital eye strain can be uncomfortable, but it does not cause lasting damage and is easily preventable.

### Do blue-cut glasses help with eye strain?

The research so far suggests they may not. The Cochrane review found there may be no short-term advantage for visual fatigue with computer use, and the AAO does not recommend special blue-light-blocking eyewear for computer use.

### Do blue-cut glasses help me sleep?

The evidence is unclear, and the Cochrane review found it inconclusive. The AAO suggests avoiding screens in the two to three hours before bed and using night mode in the evening.

### Is night mode worth using?

The AAO says nighttime settings on your devices can help minimise blue light exposure in the evening. It is a simple step you can try for free.

### Do I need blue-cut lenses if I already wear prescription glasses?

No. The right prescription for your screen distance matters far more, and blue-cut is an optional add-on that some people like. Our guide to [choosing glasses for long screen days](/blog/choosing-screen-glasses) explains the lens options.

### When should I see an eye care professional?

If your eyes are painful or red, your vision changes, or tiredness does not ease with breaks and good screen habits, see an eye care professional.
